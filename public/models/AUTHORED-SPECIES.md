# Authored cat and domestic-rabbit teaching models

These meshes are original parametric illustrations generated by
`scripts/build-species-models.mjs`. No dog meshes or third-party geometry were
copied, fitted or relabelled. Regenerate with Node.js and the project's installed
Three.js dependency: `node scripts/build-species-models.mjs`.

Each species directory contains a catalog and a binary geometry file. Catalog
offsets are bytes; positions are float32 XYZ and triangle indices are uint32.
The catalog includes bilingual labels, function, location, attachment-region,
selected innervation and recall notes, sources and a
species-specific illustrative pose configuration.

Coverage: 284 selectable pieces for Felis catus and 277 for Oryctolagus
cuniculus. Muscle coverage is 88 and 86 pieces respectively, including paired
muscles, separate heads and explicitly grouped structures. Piece counts are not
anatomical bone/muscle totals. Carpal counts are 7/9 and tarsal counts 7/6 per
limb for cat/rabbit. Selected deep muscles can be revealed using a dedicated
control. Several small bones and muscle heads remain grouped. Teeth, many muscles,
ligaments, vessels and nerves are omitted. Vertebral counts depict selected
typical variants, not every individual. The rabbit is a domestic rabbit, not
a Sylvilagus specimen.

These are NOT scans, measured reconstructions, validated attachment maps or
complete anatomical atlases. Bone surfaces, muscle volumes, attachment regions
and proportions are simplified. Pose fitting maintains limb segment lengths
but is not validated joint biomechanics. Soft-tissue deformation is illustrative.
Do not use the geometry to infer palpation or treatment locations.

References inform terminology and broad anatomical relationships, not vertex
coordinates. Source links are included in each catalog and displayed in the UI.
University of Minnesota and Penn State teaching resources inform the cat;
rabbit references include vertebral-formula and hindlimb-muscle studies linked
in its catalog. Independent veterinary anatomical review remains outstanding.
