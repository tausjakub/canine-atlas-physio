## Arrow legend: literal source meaning versus app graphics

PDF page 10 identifies a filled dot as a contact location, a solid arrow as movement with pressure and a dashed arrow as movement without pressure. These are the source's meanings, not universal anatomy conventions.

For the app:

- A ring on a 3D joint is a location marker. It must not be described as a therapist's correct contact point.
- A pale starting-position bone ghost compares positions. It is not a pressure-free arrow.
- A camera orbit changes the observer's view; it does not add an animal movement plane.
- A displayed motion plane is an added anatomical explanation. None of the audited technique pages explicitly gives its movement a formal plane name.
- If pressure/contact illustrations are later added, label them separately from bone movement and identify their source page. No force magnitude follows from arrow length.

## Independently checked veterinary conventions

Primary educational sources accessed 2026-10-22:

- [University of Minnesota veterinary anatomy: planes](https://vanat.ahc.umn.edu/anatDirections/Planes.html). Defines sagittal and median partitions, the dorsal plane relative to the back, and the transverse plane relative to the body or a limb's own long axis.
- [University of Minnesota veterinary anatomy: joint actions](https://vanat.ahc.umn.edu/anatDirections/Actions.html). Distinguishes flexion/extension, abduction/adduction and limb rotation; separates head nodding at the occipital-atlantal joint from atlantal-axial rotation.
- [University of Minnesota: veterinary versus human terminology](https://vanat.ahc.umn.edu/anatDirections/VetHuman.html). Explains the posture/orientation conflicts that prevent directly copying human head and trunk labels.
- [Elsevier: Textbook of Veterinary Anatomy, general anatomy chapter](https://elsevier-elibrary.com/contents/fullcontent/58986/epubcontent_v2/OEBPS/B9781416066071000013.htm). Primary publisher chapter text was available in web search; direct open redirected unsuccessfully. It describes limb flexion/extension as sagittal, limb abduction/adduction as transverse, and transverse cuts relative to a part's own long axis. Do not mistake the human standing-body convention for a quadruped convention.

### Geometry mapping in this particular atlas

The following table is an **implementation inference**, not wording copied from the manual. The dog asset has cranial +X, dorsal +Y and left -Z. A rigid rotation axis is perpendicular to its plane of movement.

| Reference plane | Plane in body coordinates | Normal / simple rotation axis | Appropriate explanatory use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sagittal | XY, fixed Z | Mediolateral Z | Simplified limb flexion/extension; head nodding is sagittal but needs an AO-specific pivot. |
| Median | XY at Z=0 | Mediolateral Z | Midline orientation reference. A left limb's sagittal plane is displaced from the median plane. |
| Dorsal | XZ, fixed Y | Dorsoventral Y | Lateral trunk, neck or tail bending when that motion is actually supported by the source. |
| Transverse to trunk | YZ, fixed X | Craniocaudal X | A cross-section of the trunk; a standing limb's abduction/adduction is commonly described relative to this plane. |
| Transverse to a limb segment | Perpendicular to that segment's own long axis | Segment-long axis | Axial internal/external rotation. It changes orientation with the segment and is not automatically the trunk's transverse plane. |

Rotation at the hip must therefore use a femur/segment-long axis rather than blindly rotate around global X, Y or Z. Anatomical three-dimensional motions can include coupled components; these simple explanatory axes do not reproduce measured arthrokinematics.

### Do not infer these movements

- A scapula illustration that describes tissue work does not establish a scapular bone sweep.
- Calcaneal-region alternating arrows do not establish a subtalar inversion/eversion axis.
- A patellar-region proximal glide is not a mediolateral patellar displacement test.
- A pelvic contact arrow does not prove a large pelvic rotation or sacroiliac displacement.
- The lumbar/thoracic page says body movement without naming its direction; a definite plane is unsupported.
- The cervical side-to-side page does not prove isolated C1-C2 axial rotation merely because its title includes Axis.
- Atlas contact on page 34 is not itself a head nod; nodding is explicit in the separate occiput and AO topics.
